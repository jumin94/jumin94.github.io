---
title: Education: the invisible line behind my search for a scientific language
---
I recently started to reflect more on how I got here. Mainly because I felt quite lost these past months. I was not even going to study geophysics when I first walked into the School of Astronomy and Geophysics. I didn't know what geophysics was about, but I just went in (and this is what is beautiful about public universities in Argentina) and met people willing to share their thoughts with me. There I was, getting free access to amazingly smart people willing to mentor me and chat. I remember that the first person I met was an advanced student drinking *mate* under the sun. She asked me: "Are you absolutely passionate about black holes, galaxies or supernovas? Do you imagine yourself behind a computer 90% of your time?" I honestly had no idea, but I knew that I wanted to use the privilege of being able to go to university to learn more about the world around me. After a long conversation I suddenly saw it very clearly: I wanted to be outdoors, I wanted to understand the proximate world around me, in time and spatial scales that were outside of my perception and I wanted math and physics to be my guiding lens. Geophysics it was. 

After my degree I did a PhD in Atmospheric Science. However, this path led me not only to meteorology (actually, sometimes I feel I know very little about hard-core meteorology) but also to mathematics, statistics and even a bit of philosophy. A lot of what I am sharing here came into my hands as if I were following an invisible thread that only makes sense when I look back. Along the way, people said something interesting and I looked it up. People recommended a book and I read it. I walk into libraries and pick up whatever catches my eye. So this is my education, told not as a list of degrees but as the ideas that shaped me and continue to shape me. There is an invisible line running through it (I think, I hope): a search for a scientific language that is honest about what we know, how we know it, why we want to know it and how much we should believe it. These are the threads I keep pulling.
