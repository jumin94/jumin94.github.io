South America is a highly populated continent whose economy depends heavily on agriculture, and it is particularly vulnerable to drought and heat extremes. The 2019–2022 drought showed how much people need reliable information about the next 10 to 30 years, the time scale on which water managers, farmers and policymakers actually make decisions. But near-term predictions are very uncertain: on top of the uncertainty in emissions and the disagreement between climate models, natural variability plays a huge role.

**CAUSAL-STORY** tackles this by bringing together two things I care about: *storylines* and *causal inference*. Instead of a single best guess, it builds a small set of physically plausible near-term storylines of summer drought and heat extremes, grounded in a causal model of what drives them.

The project asks three questions:

1. What are the large-scale drivers of regional drought and heat extremes in South America?
2. How will forced trends and natural variability in those drivers combine to shape future impacts?
3. How can this knowledge become actionable information for the next 10 to 30 years?

To answer them, I:

- build a causal model of the drivers, using machine learning to capture non-linear pathways;
- attribute past and projected trends to changes in the large-scale circulation and in local conditions;
- constrain existing climate simulations with what we learn about the drivers, to produce near-term storylines without the huge cost of new decadal predictions, so that the method can also be used in regions with few computing resources;
- co-produce, together with social scientists, farmers and policymakers, how these storylines are communicated, in workshops with the Climate Services group of the Barcelona Supercomputing Center and the Argentinian hub of WCRP My Climate Risk.

I am hosted by Marlene Kretschmer's Climate Causality group at the Leipzig Institute for Meteorology, with research stays planned at NOAA (with Amy Butler) and at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center (with Francisco Doblas-Reyes). Results, code and data will be openly available.
